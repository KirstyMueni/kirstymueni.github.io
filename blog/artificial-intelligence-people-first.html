Artificial Intelligence: Useful Tools, Human Responsibility

September 22, 2026 · 5 min read

What do we mean by AI?

Artificial intelligence is a broad term for computer systems designed to perform tasks associated with human capabilities, such as recognising patterns, processing language or making predictions. Generative AI is a subset that can produce new text, images, audio or code in response to instructions. These systems can be useful, but their outputs are not automatically accurate, fair or appropriate.

Where AI can help

AI tools can help people explore ideas, draft or summarise material, translate content, analyse data and automate repetitive steps. In education, a teacher might use an AI tool to brainstorm examples or prepare a first draft of a lesson resource. A learner might ask it to explain a concept in a different way. In both cases, the human remains responsible for checking the result and deciding whether it serves the learning goal.

Keep people in control

AI can produce confident-sounding errors, reflect biases in its training data and miss local context. It may also expose personal information if users paste sensitive details into a service. UNESCO's guidance on generative AI in education calls for a human-centred approach, with attention to privacy, age-appropriate use, ethical validation and meaningful learning. Read UNESCO's guidance.

Use AI thoughtfully in learning

Before using a tool, ask what task it is meant to support and whether it is suitable for the learner and context. Do not enter confidential, personal or identifying information. Check important facts against trusted sources, and be transparent when AI has made a meaningful contribution to submitted work. Follow school or workplace policies, including rules on attribution and assessment. Use AI to support thinking, not to replace the practice and judgement that build understanding.

What responsible adoption looks like

Organisations should assess tools before adopting them, explain how data is handled and provide a way for people to question or correct consequential decisions. Teachers and learners need practical guidance, not just access to software. Clear expectations help people understand what a tool can do, where it can fail and who is accountable. AI is neither a guaranteed solution nor something to fear without question. It is a set of technologies that can be helpful when used with care. The most important capabilities remain human: asking good questions, evaluating evidence, protecting people and making responsible decisions.
