Cybersecurity Starts with Everyday Habits

October 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Why small actions matter

Cybersecurity is not only a technical issue for specialists. It is the shared practice of protecting devices, accounts and information from misuse, disruption or theft. Many attacks try to persuade people to act quickly: open an unexpected attachment, share a one-time code or sign in through a link that looks familiar. A moment of verification can prevent a much larger problem. Security is most effective when people, processes and technology work together.

Build a few strong habits

Use a unique password for each important account and store passwords in a reputable password manager. Turn on multi-factor authentication wherever it is available, especially for email, banking and social media. Keep your phone, computer, browser and applications updated because updates often fix security weaknesses. Back up important files to a separate, secure location so you can recover them if a device is lost or compromised.

Pause before you trust a message

Unexpected messages that create urgency deserve extra care. Check the sender and the web address independently instead of relying on a message's display name or link. Do not share passwords, PINs or one-time verification codes in response to a call, text or email. If a request claims to come from a bank, employer or service provider, contact that organisation using a number or website you already know is genuine.

Protect information and devices

Use a screen lock and avoid leaving devices unattended while signed in. Be thoughtful about what personal or work information you post publicly. When using public Wi-Fi, avoid sensitive transactions unless you can verify the connection and use a trusted, protected service. For organisations, access should be limited to what each person needs, and staff should know how to report a suspicious message or possible incident quickly.

Respond calmly to a suspected incident

If you think an account or device has been compromised, use a different trusted device to change affected passwords, enable multi-factor authentication and sign out other sessions where possible. Preserve relevant messages or screenshots, tell the appropriate organisation or support team, and follow its incident-response instructions. Avoid deleting evidence before it can be reviewed. In Kenya, the National KE-CIRT/CC, domiciled at the Communications Authority of Kenya, coordinates cybersecurity incident response. Visit its incident reporting page or consult its alerts and advisories for current information.

Make security part of the culture

No single tool can prevent every incident. Regular updates, careful verification, backups and a culture where people can report mistakes without delay make it easier to reduce risk and recover. Cybersecurity becomes stronger when it is treated as an everyday responsibility, not a once-a-year checklist.
