Digital Literacy

Learning Analytics & Program Evaluation

Digital Literacy · ARES / Kolibri · Ol Pejeta & Lewa Conservancies

Rolling out offline learning technology is only the first step. The real measure of a digital literacy programme is whether learners actually engage with the content and whether that engagement translates into real skills. This project focused on closing that loop by turning ARES platform usage data into actionable insight.

Goal: Use platform usage logs and learner engagement data to evaluate digital literacy programmes and continuously improve how content is delivered across supported schools and communities.

The Challenge

Offline learning environments like ARES and Kolibri serve learners with little or no internet access, which makes traditional web analytics impossible. Without visibility into how devices and content were being used, it was difficult to know which resources resonated, where learners dropped off, or whether training sessions were having a lasting effect.

What I Did

  • Collected and reviewed ARES platform usage logs from deployed learning devices.
  • Tracked learner engagement patterns, including which content was accessed most and how frequently.
  • Correlated usage data with hands-on training sessions to understand the link between facilitation and independent device use.
  • Identified underused resources and surfaced them more clearly within the offline library.
  • Shared findings with facilitators and teachers to refine content curation and lesson planning.

Tools & Platforms

ARES Kolibri Usage Logs Offline Content Curation Program Evaluation

Outcomes & Impact

Analyzing engagement data made the programme more responsive to learners' real needs. Content delivery was refined based on what learners actually used, training sessions were better targeted, and facilitators gained a clearer evidence base for evaluating learning outcomes across the supported schools.

Evidence

What I Learned

Data does not have to come from the cloud to be meaningful. Even in fully offline environments, careful reading of local usage logs reveals how people learn, what they find difficult, and where support is needed most. Pairing that data with human observation on the ground made the evaluation far richer than numbers alone.
